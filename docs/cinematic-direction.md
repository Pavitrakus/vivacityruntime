# Vivacity cinematic refinement

The November 2026 direction follows the user’s latest request for a cinematic site inspired by Zenithon: a moving full-screen opening, restrained mono typography, expansive white sections, fine scientific linework, and large closing typography. Vivacity retains its own identity, product, research agenda, routes, contact flow, and functional robot demonstration. There are no invented speedups, customer logos, benchmark results, or publication claims.

## Media provenance

- `public/media/field-loop.mp4`: original procedural artwork, generated by `scripts/render-hero-field.py`. A seamless eight-second loop; abstract visual artwork, not simulation output. No third-party visual assets or code are used in its generation.
- `public/media/inferno.webm`: the user-supplied `Inferno.webm`, preserved without audio or content edits. Used as illustrative imagery, not as a claimed Vivacity result.

NASA’s media-use policy is at https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/help/. The page provides a visible source link and identifies this as external scientific visualization. It does not imply an affiliation or Vivacity result.
- Posters are frames from their respective local video files. All pre-existing image plates and editorial images remain in the repository.
- Panda meshes and their Apache 2.0 provenance remain in `public/robot/LICENSE` and `public/robot/NOTICE`.

## Final scientific studies and motion

The duplicate torus has been replaced with a model-calibration study. The left panel illustrates candidate search on a multimodal analytic objective; the right fits a damped response x(t) = exp(-c t) cos(3.8 t) to twelve explicitly synthetic observations. Each update uses only the observations currently revealed. The lower trace shows observed-minus-predicted residuals. The numerical test verifies that the fit recovers the generating damping coefficient and reduces squared residual error. These are mathematical illustrations, not Vivacity benchmark or hardware results.

The data studies repeat on a fourteen-second cycle with smoothly interpolated display states; these are not additional experiment records. The torus is a separately rendered three-dimensional wireframe, projected live onto a high-DPI canvas at up to 60 fps. Its orientation evolves continuously without a cycle reset, fade, or end-state pause. Mouse movement adds a restrained, damped camera response. Geometry is precomputed and projected each frame, with depth-sorted linework. All rendering stops outside the viewport or in hidden tabs. There are no playback controls or motion-setting button. Operating-system reduced-motion and data-saving preferences determine automatic motion; obsolete local-storage settings are ignored.

The Panda repeats its authored kinematic sequence with a short dwell at the endpoint. Selecting the actual mesh still opens the workspace; clicking the surrounding stage does not. The navigation transition uses a flat, expanding organic shape instead of a blurred gradient.

The header is full width at the top and smoothly contracts to a rectangular 62-pixel bar after scrolling (56 pixels on mobile). Controls are square. Live section backgrounds are flat; the solar study uses full-bleed footage behind the text with a flat exposure adjustment, without a gradient or backdrop blur. The earlier repeated robotics tagline has been removed.

The large footer wordmark masks a dedicated crop of the credited NASA footage inside its letters. `solar-detail.mp4` and its poster are derivatives of `solar-flows.mp4`: crop 360 x 140 at (460,145), retained at the native crop resolution to avoid redundant upscaling. This tighter crop excludes the visualization frame and uses no generated measurements. Credits remain linked in both the research film and footer.

Existing orbital and flow plates are reused on the contact and partnership pages as explicitly illustrative images. The original research image, routes, contact behavior, and scientific agenda remain intact.
